%% ----- Abstract ---------------------------------------------------------
% \chapter*{Abstract}
% \addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

% Saturn's moon \titan{} is unique amongst solar system bodies in simultaneously
% possessing a dense nitrogen-dominated atmosphere, a global methane--ethane
% precipitation and lake cycle that is structurally analogous to the terrestrial
% hydrological cycle, the largest inventory of complex organic chemistry in the
% solar system outside Earth, confirmed polar hydrocarbon seas containing an
% estimated $\sim$70{,}000\,km$^3$ of liquid methane--ethane--nitrogen, and a
% deep interior water--ammonia ocean inferred from tidal deformation
% measurements.  This convergence of chemical feedstock, cycling solvent, and
% liquid environments makes \titan{} not merely a potentially habitable world
% but a natural laboratory for prebiotic chemistry at planetary scale.

% This thesis presents a quantitative, spatially-resolved probabilistic
% framework for assessing \titan{}'s surface and near-surface habitability at
% five temporal configurations spanning the Late Heavy Bombardment
% ($-3.5\gya$), a lake-formation transition epoch ($-1.0\gya$), the
% present-day \cassini{} epoch, a near-future solar warming window ($+0.25\gya$),
% and the red-giant future ($+5.9\gya$).  Eight observational
% habitability-proxy features are derived from the complete \cassini{} dataset
% --- synthetic aperture radar (SAR), near-infrared spectroscopy (VIMS),
% thermal mapping (CIRS), radar altimetry, and the Lopes et al.\ (2019) global
% geomorphological map --- and combined through a Bayesian Beta-conjugate
% posterior update to yield probability maps $P(\text{habitable}\mid\featurevec)$
% at \SI{4490}{\metre\per\text{px}} resolution across the full globe.

% At the present epoch, habitability is highest along the margins of the north
% polar hydrocarbon seas, where posterior means reach $P(H)\approx0.55$--0.57.
% During the Late Heavy Bombardment, impact-melt ponds at major crater sites
% constituted the dominant habitability zones.  In the far future, solar
% red-giant expansion will produce a near-globally habitable water--ammonia
% ocean over an extraordinary accumulated organic substrate.

% The analysis independently validates the NASA \dragonfly{} mission's selection
% of Selk crater: although its present-day solvent habitability is modest
% (the $\sim$6th percentile of present-epoch and $\sim$40th percentile of
% past-epoch habitability), its geomorphological diversity is the highest of any
% equatorial site ($6.8\times$ the global median) --- the Cassini proxy for the
% past water--organic chemistry that \dragonfly{} targets.  The instrument suite
% is shown to be well-matched to the dominant habitability drivers at the target site.
